# Spoilers

**Stop here if you'd rather find them yourself.** This is the record of the rare and unexplained
things in the scene, so future changes don't break them. Code: `renderers/procedural/mystery.py`.
Everything is decided by the date (and the forecast), so a given day always renders the same way.

## Rare events

| What | When | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A comet | Every year, for 7-11 nights from a date picked by the year; 40% of years have a second one. It sits low in the evening twilight (60%) or the morning twilight, near the sun's side of the sky. Brightest mid-run. Its tail points away from the sun. | Left sky for morning comets, right sky for evening ones, clear of the tower |
| The ghost ship | On nights when there is fog before 05:00 or after 22:00, half the time, for 45 minutes starting between 00:36 and 04:12 | A pale three-master drifting left to right across the bay beyond the lamp, glowing faintly through the fog, with a green stern light and no reflection |
| A whale | Three days a year (picked by the year, between June and October), for five minutes between 09:00 and 17:00 | In the bay: a blow, the back rolling, the blow again, the flukes, and then just a slick |
| The minute | 27 September, 08:57 (the scene's first morning, v1 was finished then) | The tower door stands open with golden light spilling down the path |
| The clock eclipse | About 19 November at 08:24 CET and about 22-23 January at 08:50 CET (pure astronomy, every year) | The sun passes directly behind the tower clock, and the dial lights up from behind |

## Unexplained presences

| What | When | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The lantern | One night in twelve, for 40 minutes starting between 03:00 and 03:15 | A warm light walking the far shore, with its reflection |
| The cottage window | One night in twenty, for 12-42 minutes starting between 01:36 and 03:12 | A flickering light in the empty cottage on the outer islet (far left) |
| The footprints | On days with lying snow, about a third of the time, appearing at some minute between 00:30 and 05:00 | A line of prints from the gap in the wall out across the field, ending in the middle of nowhere. Fresh snow fills them in |

## Not mysteries, just real

- The telescope tracks the moon when it's up and lit, otherwise the brightest planet, otherwise
  the brightest star well up. The dome only opens on clear nights.
- The weathervane points into the forecast wind; the tide follows the moon, with springs and neaps.
- Meteors follow the real showers (Quadrantids, Lyrids, Eta Aquariids, Perseids, Orionids, Leonids,
  Geminids) and fly away from their radiants.
